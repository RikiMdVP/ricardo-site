Who Pays and Where? Local Housing Adjustments to Immigration in Spain

with Wladimir Cerda-Cerda

Working paper — EPAINOS Award candidate, presented at ERSA 2026

Using a shift-share instrument built on predetermined 2005 settlement patterns and an exogenous spatial-pull control, this paper identifies the causal effect of immigration on local housing prices across 699 Spanish municipalities (2014–2025). A 1pp increase in the net immigration rate raises housing prices by ~0.73% in the short run, accumulating to ~2.25% in the long run. Housing supply is inelastic and shows no significant response; the effect concentrates in non-urban municipalities, where immigration-driven firm creation and income gains induce native co-location, compounding demand pressure over time.

The Economic Costs of Parliamentary Populism: Evidence from European Regions

with Roberto Balado-Naves and Alberto Díaz-Dapena

Working paper — Presented at ERSA 2026 — slides

Difference-GMM estimation on 239 NUTS2 regions across 30 European countries (1996–2022), combining ARDECO regional accounts with the PopuList database, to trace how episodes of political unrest feed back into regional economic growth. The analysis pairs impulse response functions with spatial-autocorrelation diagnostics (Moran's I) to characterise how these costs propagate across neighbouring regions.

Immigration and Local Economic Resistance

with Wladimir Antonio Cerda, Guillermo Rodríguez-López and André Carrascal Incera

Working paper

This paper studies whether immigration strengthens or weakens the resilience of local labour markets in Spain. Using a panel of 753 municipalities (population above 10,000) over 2001–2021, we measure local employment resistance as the gap between a municipality's employment growth and the national benchmark, and instrument the change in the local immigrant rate with a Card-style shift-share instrument based on 1991 continental settlement patterns. 2SLS estimates show that immigration causally increases local employment resistance, driven mainly by retail, other services and manufacturing, with no evidence of native displacement — municipalities receiving more immigrants also retain or attract more native population. The effect is concentrated in the 2001–2011 decade and is broadly consistent across urban/rural status, sectoral concentration, distance to core areas, and initial sectoral specialization, pointing to local economic complementarity rather than competition between immigrants and natives.

Immigration and Housing Prices in Madrid's Functional Urban Area

Working paper

Building on Saiz and Wachter (2011), this paper studies 166 municipalities in Madrid's Functional Urban Area (2003–2024) using notarial housing-price statistics and a dual instrumental-variable strategy — a spatial-diffusion gravity instrument combined with a Bartik shift-share instrument — to identify the effect of immigration on local housing prices.

COVID-19 as a Preference Shock: Human Capital Gaps Between Metropolitan and Left-behind Spanish Regions

Working paper

A difference-in-differences event-study design uses university admission cut-off grades (notas de corte) to test whether the COVID-19 pandemic acted as a preference shock, widening or narrowing human-capital gaps between metropolitan and peripheral Spanish universities.
