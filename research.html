The Economic Costs of Parliamentary Populism: Evidence from European Regions

with Roberto Balado-Naves and Alberto Díaz-Dapena

Under review at Regional Studies — presented at ERSA 2026

System-GMM estimation on 239 NUTS2 regions across 30 European countries (1996–2022), combining ARDECO regional accounts with the PopuList database, to trace how episodes of political unrest feed back into regional economic growth. The analysis pairs impulse response functions with spatial-autocorrelation diagnostics (Moran's I) to characterise how these costs propagate across neighbouring regions. The Populist Trap: Development Traps and Populist Rhetorical Contagion

Working paper

A supervised text classifier applied to Spanish regional parliamentary debates (2014–2025) traces the contagion of populist rhetoric from VOX to the Partido Popular. A staggered event-study design, with a leave-one-out instrument for VOX vote share and Callaway–Sant'Anna estimators, is used to address the endogeneity of local development-trap conditions. Who Pays and Where? Local Housing Adjustments to Immigration in Spain

with Wladimir Cerda-Cerda

Working paper — EPAINOS Award candidate, presented at ERSA 2026

Municipal-level analysis of how immigration inflows relate to diverging housing sale prices and volumes across Spain, using provincial salary data as an income proxy and exploring causal-forest methods to capture heterogeneous effects across municipality types. Immigration and Housing Prices in Madrid's Functional Urban Area

Working paper

Building on Saiz and Wachter (2011), this paper studies 166 municipalities in Madrid's Functional Urban Area (2003–2024) using notarial housing-price statistics and a dual instrumental-variable strategy — a spatial-diffusion gravity instrument combined with a Bartik shift-share instrument — to identify the effect of immigration on local housing prices. COVID-19 as a Preference Shock: Human Capital Gaps Between Metropolitan and Left-behind Spanish Regions

Working paper

A difference-in-differences event-study design uses university admission cut-off grades (notas de corte) to test whether the COVID-19 pandemic acted as a preference shock, widening or narrowing human-capital gaps between metropolitan and peripheral Spanish universities.
